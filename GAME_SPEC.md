# TopWar — Game Spec

## Core loop

Steer a tiny auto-firing squad through an endless deterministic enemy stream. Side rewards ask the player to divert fire while all seven enemies in each row remain. Soldiers merge into stronger tiers; giant Bosses mark each tier handoff. The run ends when the visible squad reaches zero, followed by immediate Retry. The player advances in simulation Z while staying near the bottom of the portrait view; normal enemies remain stationary in gameplay coordinates and visually walk toward the squad.

## Formula-driven tiers

Level 001 authors a first transition start at row **48**, **96** transition rows, **96** stable rows, and quadratic probability. The cycle is 192 rows: the transition into Tier N starts at `48 + (N - 2) × 192` and fully saturates 96 rows later. During a transition only the established tier and its next tier coexist. The center-most slot guarantees a reveal on the exact start row. Slot rolls use the run seed mixed with the authored stream seed, independently of gameplay RNG. Every initial run and Retry gets a fresh browser-crypto seed; a snapshot retains its run seed. There is no authored tier maximum.

Normal enemy HP and player rifle damage use the same committed bootstrap: Tier-1 = **3**, Tier-2 = **300**. Above Tier-2, enemy HP and rifle power each multiply by **10** per tier by default; the two multipliers can be tuned independently at runtime. Normal enemies all use radius **0.30** and the same gameplay size. The Kenney body texture, skin, clothing, leather, and shoes stay fixed across enemy tiers; only the compact rounded helmet and vest change color. Enemy gear cycles through coral red, orange-red, gold, violet, rose, and lime. Player clothing is blue and helmets cycle through five blues. Weapon and projectile visuals stay bounded rather than growing forever.

## Player combat and defense

Level 001 starts with one Tier-1 rifle soldier. Every **10** rifle soldiers of a tier automatically merge into one of the next tier, cascading as needed. Only the highest two adjacent rifle tiers remain active, visible, and firing. Lower-tier leftover exchange value is preserved exactly as a non-firing rifle remainder: it contributes to defense and future normalization, but not visible count or offense. If casualties lower the highest active tier, lower-tier bodies may become visible again. All tiers share the authored rifle fire rate, projectile speed, and range. A Tier N soldier has `10^(N - 1)` Tier-1-equivalent defense points. A Tier N projectile has the same penetration budget: each lower-tier normal enemy costs its tier's defense value, then the shot continues while budget remains. Same-tier or higher-tier normal enemies stop the shot; every Boss stops it. Casualties consume rifle defense first and can demote a high-tier body into lower-tier bodies. Rocket specialists remain last-loss prototype infrastructure and are not Level 001's current progression route.

## Rewards

Every eight-row block has exactly one deterministic, random-looking side reward at X = **±3.2**. The live panel may temporarily change this to one per N rows; it remains guaranteed block placement, never a Bernoulli chance. Reward lookahead is **30** world units; enemy lookahead remains **96**. Rewards do not replace enemies. A reward's tier follows the highest fully saturated enemy tier: Tier-1 before row 144, Tier-2 from row 144, Tier-3 from row 336, and so on by formula. Every rifle tier can progress every reward tier by one hit; each valid hit consumes the projectile, making reward pursuit an offensive tradeoff. Ten hits grant one soldier of the reward tier and normalize the squad. Rockets pass through without reward progress. Ignored rewards expire harmlessly. The golden world-space crate carries the actual tier-colored player helmet on its front and shows damage through a durability bar, cracks, hit wobble, and a near-break flash. Generic side-armory code exists but is inactive in Level 001.

## Boss handoffs

A Boss of Tier N replaces one normal row eight rows before Tier N+1 fully saturates. The Tier-1 Boss is row **136**, with HP `3 × 4000 = 12,000`. Each later Boss has HP equal to its tier's normal power times **1000**. Bosses share visual scale **7** and collision radius **2.0** at every tier, using the same cycling enemy palette as their normal tier. Boss contact or defense-line crossing engages a stationary showdown: forward progression and both streams stop, while steering and auto-fire continue. After a 0.6-second wind-up, the Boss slams for exactly ten Tier N+1 soldiers' exchange value, then repeats every 2 seconds until it dies or the squad reaches zero. Casualties consume the lowest active rifle tier first, with exact higher-tier demotion; rockets remain last-loss. Boss death resumes progression on the next tick. Bosses have no special rewards.

## Presentation

The top HUD displays the highest enemy tier introduced at the player's current progression row. Desktop steering uses A/D or Left/Right; touch and pen use dragging. P or Space pauses and resumes gameplay and presentation animation; Escape opens or closes TUNE without pausing. A compact desktop tuning panel exposes bullet speed, bullet range, reward density, enemy HP growth, rifle power growth, fire rate, horizontal move speed, and forward speed. Its values are temporary: Retry keeps them, Reset Defaults restores authored values, and page reload starts from the JSON defaults.

The battlefield is a large bridge over a muted sea. Distant enemy ranks dissolve into war haze ahead of layered, damaged port and industrial silhouettes with smoke and restrained fire glow. Players are blue-clothed Kenney toy soldiers with rimmed toy steel helmets, compact vests, forward-pointing charcoal rifles, rifle-only firing recoil, and a muzzle flash; rifle-kind simulation projectiles appear as slim bright tracers. Unarmed enemy soldiers face the player and use four baked running poses with a slight waddle and bounce. Hit feedback briefly flashes gear yellow; fallen grunts turn gray, float upward, and fade with a small burst. Enemy rendered size is 0.82 of the baked model while gameplay collision stays unchanged. Bosses are giant soldiers of the same family with proportionate helmets, Tier-colored tactical plates with dark straps and pouches, a floating framed HP plate with its own subtle hit tick, a brief translucent warm whole-body hit wash, slower four-pose locomotion, hit squash, and chunky death tumble. Reward hits pulse, reward removal and recruited soldiers pop, and tier-ups glow with a short ring. Player damage flashes red. Web Audio synthesizes throttled rifle shots, rockets, damage/Game Over cues, reward-hit ticks, reward-acquisition chimes, throttled Boss-hit thuds, and throttled enemy-death yelps. A completed reward produces both a hit tick and acquisition chime. Browser audio begins after the first pointer or keyboard interaction.

Incoming normal contact and Boss slam damage flashes the affected blue soldiers bright red, then briefly knocks removed visible members away. The specific normal attacker flashes yellow and pops out; projectile kills retain the gray-rise-fade death. A fatal knockout finishes before the Game Over overlay appears. TUNE can temporarily multiply Boss HP from 0.25× to 100× for melee playtests; the authored HP progression is unchanged and the runtime default is 3×.

## Development principles

Keep deterministic fixed-step gameplay separate from disposable rendering and audio. Load balance and progression inputs at runtime. Serialize plain gameplay state, including generic tiers and stream cursors. Prioritize fast iteration and long-run playtest evidence before tuning the infinite loop.
