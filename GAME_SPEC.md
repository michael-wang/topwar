# TopWar — Game Spec

## Core loop

Steer a tiny auto-firing squad through an endless deterministic enemy stream. Soldiers merge into stronger tiers; giant Bosses mark each tier handoff. The run ends when the visible squad reaches zero, followed by immediate Retry. The player advances in simulation Z while staying near the bottom of the portrait view.

## Current prototype experiment: Catharsis attack corridors

This build tests larger, readable enemies and lane decisions. It does not adopt the full Catharsis progression design. Five runtime-configured corridors replace Level 001's seven-wide rows. One or two lanes hold priority for three groups; each four-enemy group arrives over seven row slots, with a possible Heavy replacing its first Grunt. Remaining slots are quiet. Grunts have exactly 1 HP and approach at 0.8 units/second; Heavies have 5 HP and approach at 0.3. Rifle damage against these archetypes is expressed in Tier-1 rifle-hit units, while Boss damage retains existing tier power. Grunts render at scale 1.4 (previously 0.82); amber-armored Heavies render at 1.89. Both retain the 0.30 arcade collision radius. Continuous drag stays operational; full steer aligns with the outer corridor.

Temporary reinforcement crates occupy a quiet lane in their local pressure block. Only shots landing while the squad center is within 0.5 units of that lane can progress them. The initial density is one crate per 12-row block; ignored crates still expire harmlessly. Merge, tier power, Boss cadence and progression remain prototype infrastructure. Higher-tier rifles can erase Heavies quickly, and contact/leak damage still uses retained tier exchange value. Evaluate these limitations during playtest rather than interpreting them as the final difficulty design. See `CATHARSIS_EXPERIMENT.md` for tuning and verification notes.

## Formula-driven tiers

Level 001 authors a first transition start at row **48**, **96** transition rows, **96** stable rows, and quadratic probability. The cycle is 192 rows: the transition into Tier N starts at `48 + (N - 2) × 192` and fully saturates 96 rows later. During a transition only the established tier and its next tier coexist. The first generated slot guarantees a reveal on the exact start row. Slot rolls use the run seed mixed with the authored stream seed, independently of gameplay RNG. Every initial run and Retry gets a fresh browser-crypto seed; a snapshot retains its run seed. There is no authored tier maximum.

The retained tier-power bootstrap is Tier-1 = **3**, Tier-2 = **300**, then **10** times per tier by default. This still drives rifle and Boss balance; normal experiment enemies use their archetype HP instead. The Kenney body texture, skin, clothing, leather, and shoes stay fixed across enemy tiers. Grunt gear cycles through blood crimson, toxic olive, bruised violet, rust orange, gunmetal, and dirty mustard; Heavy gear stays amber to identify the role. Player clothing is blue and helmets cycle through five blues. Weapon and projectile visuals stay bounded rather than growing forever.

## Player combat and defense

Level 001 starts with one Tier-1 rifle soldier. Every **10** rifle soldiers of a tier automatically merge into one of the next tier, cascading as needed. Only the highest two adjacent rifle tiers remain active, visible, and firing. Lower-tier leftover exchange value is preserved exactly as a non-firing rifle remainder: it contributes to defense and future normalization, but not visible count or offense. If casualties lower the highest active tier, lower-tier bodies may become visible again. All tiers share the authored rifle fire rate, projectile speed, and range. Higher-tier shots gain a bounded normal-enemy hit corridor (0.45 radius per tier after Tier-1, capped at a 0.90 bonus) to offset fewer firing bodies; rewards and Bosses keep their normal targeting widths. A Tier N soldier has `10^(N - 1)` Tier-1-equivalent defense points. A Tier N projectile has the same penetration budget: each lower-tier normal enemy costs its tier's defense value, then the shot continues while budget remains. Same-tier or higher-tier normal enemies stop the shot; every Boss stops it. Casualties consume rifle defense first and can demote a high-tier body into lower-tier bodies. Rocket specialists remain last-loss prototype infrastructure and are not Level 001's current progression route.

## Rewards

The temporary lane reward uses guaranteed deterministic block placement, never a Bernoulli chance. TUNE can change it to one per N rows. Reward lookahead is **30** world units; enemy lookahead remains **96**. Rewards do not replace enemies. A reward's tier follows the highest fully saturated enemy tier: Tier-1 before row 144, Tier-2 from row 144, Tier-3 from row 336, and so on by formula. Every rifle tier can progress every reward tier by one hit while aligned with its corridor; each valid hit consumes the projectile. Ten hits grant one soldier of the reward tier and normalize the squad. Rockets pass through without reward progress. Ignored rewards expire harmlessly. Existing crate presentation and inactive generic side-armory infrastructure are retained for this experiment.

## Boss handoffs

A Boss of Tier N replaces one normal row eight rows before Tier N+1 fully saturates. The Tier-1 Boss is row **136**, with HP `3 × 4000 = 12,000`. Each later Boss has HP equal to its tier's normal power times **1000**. Bosses share visual scale **7** and collision radius **2.0** at every tier, using the same cycling enemy palette as their normal tier. Boss contact or defense-line crossing engages a stationary showdown: forward progression and both streams stop, while steering and auto-fire continue. After a 0.6-second wind-up, the Boss slams for exactly ten Tier N+1 soldiers' exchange value, then repeats every 2 seconds until it dies or the squad reaches zero. Casualties consume the lowest active rifle tier first, with exact higher-tier demotion; rockets remain last-loss. Boss death resumes progression on the next tick. Bosses have no special rewards.

## Presentation

The top HUD displays the highest enemy tier introduced at the player's current progression row. Desktop steering uses A/D or Left/Right; touch and pen use dragging. P or Space pauses and resumes gameplay and presentation animation; Escape opens or closes TUNE without pausing. A compact desktop tuning panel exposes bullet speed, bullet range, reward density, Boss base HP growth, rifle power growth, fire rate, horizontal move speed, forward speed, Boss HP scale, and music volume. Its values are temporary: Retry keeps them, Reset Defaults restores defaults, and page reload starts from defaults.

The battlefield is a large bridge over a muted sea. Distant enemy ranks dissolve into war haze ahead of layered, damaged port and industrial silhouettes, with an open central approach. Faint cranes, control structures, and transports reveal the occupied beachhead supplying the army. Asymmetric burning wrecks and near, middle, and far artillery impacts add dark smoke. Deep inferno surges intermittently backlight the occupied port sky and rising smoke. Warships fade at the sea edges and pass behind the solid bridge; faint aircraft silhouettes, sometimes in pairs, and small flak bursts animate the distant sky. Distant thunder-like ordnance sounds occur independently of visible flashes. Soft contact shadows ground the squad, enemies, Boss, and rewards. Players are blue-clothed Kenney toy soldiers with rimmed toy steel helmets, compact vests, forward-pointing charcoal rifles, rifle-only firing recoil, and a muzzle flash; rifle-kind simulation projectiles appear as slim bright tracers. Unarmed enemy soldiers face the player and use four baked running poses with a slight waddle and bounce. Hit feedback briefly flashes gear yellow; fallen grunts turn gray, float upward, and fade with a small burst. The experiment uses runtime-configured enemy model size while keeping the existing arcade collision radius. Bosses are giant soldiers of the same family with proportionate helmets, Tier-colored tactical plates with dark straps and pouches, a floating framed HP plate with its own subtle hit tick, a brief translucent warm whole-body hit wash, slower four-pose locomotion, hit squash, and a gray upright death cry before a forward collapse and fade. Reward hits pulse, reward removal and recruited soldiers pop, and tier-ups glow with a short ring. Player damage flashes red. Web Audio synthesizes distinct, rhythmically varied rifle shots, quiet normal enemy yelps, armored Boss hits, a Boss death cry and collapse, player damage, rockets, Game Over cues, reward-hit ticks, and reward-acquisition chimes. A completed reward produces both a hit tick and acquisition chime. An original dark procedural synth score builds evolving harmony, bass phrases, and sparse motifs beneath combat, adds tension for Boss approach and showdown, and ducks for Boss death; TUNE controls Music volume. Browser audio begins after the first pointer or keyboard interaction.

Incoming normal contact and Boss slam damage flashes the affected blue soldiers bright red, then briefly knocks removed visible members away. The specific normal attacker flashes yellow and pops out; projectile kills retain the gray-rise-fade death. A fatal knockout finishes before the Game Over overlay appears. TUNE can temporarily multiply Boss HP from 0.25× to 100× for melee playtests; the authored HP progression is unchanged and the runtime default is 3×.

## Development principles

Keep deterministic fixed-step gameplay separate from disposable rendering and audio. Load balance and progression inputs at runtime. Serialize plain gameplay state, including generic tiers and stream cursors. Prioritize fast iteration and long-run playtest evidence before tuning the infinite loop.
